AI DEPLOYMENT DECISION ENGINE
FULL PRODUCT VISION (not MVP scope — long-term reference)

This document captures the full product thesis and where the MVP sits
inside it. The MVP specs (v1-v4) are a deliberately constrained slice
of this. Nothing here is scoped for the current build — kept
separate so the MVP docs stay lean and this stays the place to check
"where does this fit in the bigger picture."


1. CORE THESIS

Not an AI ROI calculator. An AI-assisted deployment decision system
answering: "Should we deploy AI here, and if so, what is the
economically and operationally sensible way to do it?"

Core principle: AI capability != AI suitability. A technically
solvable problem is not necessarily an economically worthwhile AI
problem. The system must be capable of steering away from AI, and — as
of MVP spec v4 — even capable of declining to prescribe an answer at
all, instead surfacing the facts a human needs to apply their own
judgment.


2. THE FULL WORKFLOW (MVP builds one vertical slice of this)

Business Problem
  -> AI Interviewer (adaptive, state-driven)
  -> Structured Process Model
  -> Identify Missing Inputs
  -> Sector Benchmark Cross-Reference
  -> External Data Research (live, in the full product; static in MVP)
  -> Evidence + Assumptions (5-tag provenance system)
  -> Economic / Operational Model
       -> Current Process baseline
       -> AI Alternatives (multiple architectures compared, not just
          one proposed approach)
  -> Scenario Analysis (multi-variable, not just 2-3 sensitivity
     variables)
  -> Scored, ranked Decision Drivers (not a forced category)
  -> Cited Decision Report


3. WHAT THE MVP INTENTIONALLY LEAVES OUT

- Only 2 sectors (full product: broad sector coverage, ideally
  user-extensible sector packs rather than a hardcoded pair)
- Static benchmark packs (full product: live-updated benchmark
  sourcing with a proper source registry and refresh cadence)
- No persistence (full product: saved assessments, revisit/update
  over time, track how estimates compared to actual outcomes)
- No multi-architecture comparison (full product: compare several
  concrete AI architectures side by side — e.g. small fine-tuned model
  vs. RAG+LLM vs. agentic system — each separately costed, not just
  one proposed approach plus qualitative alternatives)
- No organization-specific data integration (full product: pull real
  salary/infra numbers from the org's own systems rather than
  benchmarks/user estimates)
- No feedback loop (full product: capture actual pilot results and
  post-deployment measurements, use them to calibrate future
  estimates — this is the piece that would make the system's ranges
  get more honest over time instead of staying static)
- Single-session tool (full product: multi-user, org accounts, shared
  assessment history)


4. PRODUCT DECISIONS MADE DURING SCOPING (kept for the record)

These are decisions made while narrowing the original brief into a
buildable MVP — documented here since they reflect real product
judgment, separate from pure scope-cutting:

4.1 Scores explain, they don't decide.
Economic/Feasibility/Risk scores are indicators for a human to read,
not inputs to a formula that spits out a category. This was a
deliberate move away from "the system decides" toward "the system
informs."

4.2 No prescriptive recommendation at all.
Taken further than 4.1 — the system was originally going to output
BUILD/PILOT/DON'T BUILD, then moved to a scored composite, then
finally to Decision Drivers with no label at all. The reasoning: a
recommendation label implies the system knows the organization's risk
tolerance, budget flexibility, and strategic priorities, which it
doesn't. Ranked facts plus an explicit "here's the biggest
uncertainty" respects that boundary instead of pretending to resolve
it.

4.3 Benchmarks are cross-checks, never silent inputs.
Public sector data is useful for sanity-checking a user's numbers, not
for quietly filling gaps in a way that launders industry averages
into what looks like company-specific data. The user is always asked
explicitly when a benchmark would be used to fill a gap.

4.4 Ranges over false precision, everywhere.
Applies to LLM-estimated numbers (automation rate, accuracy,
integration complexity) and to engineering effort (bands, not exact
hours). This runs through the whole system, not just one section — it
is the practical expression of the "don't let the LLM invent
financial certainty" principle from the original brief.

4.5 Five-tag provenance system.
user_provided / sourced / estimated / assumed / derived. Every number
in every output traces to exactly one of these. This is the
transparency mechanism the entire product's credibility rests on —
without it, this is just another confident-sounding calculator.


5. LONGER-TERM EXTENSIONS (unchanged from original brief, still valid)

- More industries and process templates, ideally as an extensible
  pack format rather than hardcoded sectors
- Larger, live source registry with proper refresh/versioning
- Real-time cloud/model pricing lookups
- Organization-specific salary and infrastructure data integration
- More detailed workforce-impact modeling (redeployment vs. reduction,
  second-order effects)
- Probabilistic feasibility models (proper distributions instead of
  simple ranges)
- Actual pilot-result feedback loop
- Post-deployment measurement and continuous ROI tracking
- Integration with enterprise planning/budgeting systems
- Side-by-side comparison of multiple implementation architectures
- Automatic generation of pilot plans (what to validate, how, by when)


6. HOW TO USE THIS DOCUMENT

This is a reference, not a build target. When the MVP write-up asks
"what would you build next and why," this document is the answer —
pull directly from sections 3 and 5. When scoping any future
iteration, check section 4 first so product decisions already reasoned
through don't get silently reversed by a new feature request.
